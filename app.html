Data Sources

Fire detection data from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), covering 2000–2024 across 195 countries worldwide.

  • MODIS (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) — Terra (2000–present) and Aqua (2002–present) satellites. 1km spatial resolution, 1–2 day revisit.
  • VIIRS (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) — Suomi NPP (2012–present). 375m spatial resolution, higher detection sensitivity.

Harmonization Approach

VIIRS detects significantly more fires than MODIS due to its higher spatial resolution and thermal sensitivity. Naively summing detections from both sensors creates an artificial spike in 2012 when VIIRS came online.

We compute per-country harmonization ratios by comparing MODIS and VIIRS detections during their overlap period (2012–2024). The ratio is the overall total of VIIRS detections divided by total MODIS detections across all overlap months (minimum 6 months required):

Harmonized = MODIS_count × ratio + VIIRS_count

This scales MODIS counts upward to match VIIRS sensitivity, producing a consistent time series across the full 2000–2024 period.

Confidence Filtering

Each detection carries a confidence value. High confidence filtering uses:

  • MODIS: numeric confidence ≥ 80 (range 0–100)
  • VIIRS: categorical confidence = "high" (low/nominal/high)

High-confidence detections represent ~15% of total detections, filtering out cloud edges, hot industrial sites, and sun glint.

Limitations

  • Cloud cover causes undercounting, especially in tropical regions during wet season
  • Satellite revisit gaps mean some fires are missed entirely
  • MODIS pixel size (1km²) can merge multiple small fires into one detection
  • Harmonization ratios assume stable sensor relationship over the overlap period
  • FRP (Fire Radiative Power) values are instantaneous measurements, not total fire energy
